Email Security Checker

Check public SPF, DMARC and MX signals for a domain. This helps identify common email-domain configuration gaps; it is not an email penetration test.

Why SPF and DMARC matter

SPF and DMARC are public DNS signals used in email authentication. A missing or weak policy can make domain email governance harder and increase spoofing risk. The scanner reports what is publicly observable.

How to interpret the results

SPF identifies which mail systems are permitted to send messages for a domain. A record should be reviewed against the organisation's actual senders; adding multiple SPF records or exceeding DNS lookup limits can create failures.

DMARC tells receiving systems how to handle messages that fail domain-alignment checks and can provide reporting. A policy of p=none is useful for observation but does not request quarantine or rejection. Moving to enforcement should follow sender inventory and report review.

MX records identify the mail exchangers for a domain. Their presence alone does not prove that mailboxes, authentication or delivery are configured correctly.

What this checker cannot prove

This is a public DNS check, not a mailbox compromise investigation, phishing simulation, mail-server penetration test or deliverability guarantee. It cannot inspect private tenant settings, message content, mailbox access controls or every third-party sender. DNS propagation and resolver differences can also affect results.

Recommended next steps

  1. Inventory every legitimate sender, including website forms, CRM, billing and marketing platforms.
  2. Consolidate SPF into one valid record and confirm all authorised senders are included.
  3. Publish DMARC reporting and review alignment failures before enforcing a stricter policy.
  4. Confirm DKIM signing for each sending service and remove obsolete DNS entries.
  5. Retest after DNS changes and monitor reports over a representative sending period.
