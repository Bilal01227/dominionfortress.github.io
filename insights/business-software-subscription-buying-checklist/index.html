Buying Business Software Subscriptions: A Practical Checklist

Published 24 November 2026 · Dominion Fortress

A low headline price does not automatically make a software subscription good value. Before buying, confirm the product fits the workflow, the licence permits the intended use and the account can be administered safely over time.

1. Start with the job the software must do

List the workflow, users, required features and current alternatives. Separate must-have requirements from optional features. Avoid buying several overlapping tools when one existing platform already covers the job.

2. Check licence and user limits

Confirm whether the plan is for an individual, team, business or education user. Review seat limits, device limits, commercial-use permissions, sharing restrictions and rules for contractors or external collaborators. Do not assume a personal plan can be used by a company.

3. Verify region, currency and billing terms

Check whether the plan is available in the buyer's country, which currency is charged, whether taxes apply, and whether billing is monthly or annual. Review renewal dates, cancellation windows, refunds and price-change terms before committing.

4. Review security and data handling

For tools that process company data, review access controls, MFA, audit logs, data retention, export options, deletion, subprocessors and the vendor's security documentation. Avoid placing sensitive business information in a tool without approval.

5. Plan ownership and continuity

Use a company-controlled email address where appropriate, assign at least two trusted administrators for critical tools and document renewal ownership. Make sure the business can export its data and recover access if an employee leaves.

6. Compare total cost—not just list price

Include seats, add-ons, storage, API usage, taxes, currency conversion, support tiers and renewal pricing. A discounted first term may become expensive at renewal. Ask for a written quote when plan availability or regional billing is unclear.

7. Keep procurement transparent

Purchase through authorized channels and retain invoices or receipts. Confirm the exact plan, term, region and account owner before payment. Never accept shared accounts, unclear licence provenance or offers that require bypassing vendor controls.
