Workflow Automation Consulting: Questions to Answer Before Building

Published 24 November 2026 · Dominion Fortress

Automation projects fail when teams start with a tool before understanding the process. A short discovery exercise can show whether a workflow should be automated, redesigned, or left manual.

1. Choose a process with a clear owner

Name the person accountable for the workflow and the team that performs it. Record how often it runs, how long it takes, what delays it and what happens when it fails. Without an owner, there is no one to validate the proposed change.

2. Map the normal path and exceptions

Document each input, decision, handoff, approval and output. Include missing data, duplicates, rework, urgent requests and unusual cases. The exception path often determines the real complexity more than the happy path.

3. Inventory systems and data access

List the forms, spreadsheets, databases, APIs, email inboxes and business applications involved. Confirm who owns each system, whether supported integrations exist and what permissions can be granted. Do not plan around credentials or access the customer cannot legitimately provide.

4. Decide whether AI is actually needed

Use deterministic rules for predictable conditions. Use AI where language, documents or ambiguous information need interpretation, and keep validation or human review around uncertain or consequential decisions. A model is not a substitute for authorization.

5. Define failure handling and controls

Specify timeouts, retries, duplicate prevention, logging, alerts, manual override and rollback. Decide which actions are read-only, which change records and which communicate externally. Require human approval for high-impact actions.

6. Estimate value and operating cost

Measure current cycle time, error rates, manual effort and delay costs. Estimate implementation, API, hosting, maintenance and review costs. Set a realistic success threshold and identify what must be measured during a pilot.

7. Pilot before broad rollout

Test representative cases, including edge cases and failures. Compare results with the existing process, train users and document ownership. Expand only when the pilot meets agreed quality, safety and operational criteria.
