External Attack Surface Assessment: Find Internet-Facing Exposure

An external attack surface assessment maps assets that an organization exposes to the internet and highlights unexpected services, stale records and observable security weaknesses.

Scope and assets

Domain and subdomain inventory: review approved root domains, subdomains and related DNS records.

Testing approach

Certificates and DNS: inspect certificate transparency observations, DNS configuration and potential asset relationships.

Evidence and validation

Internet-facing services: identify in-scope exposed hosts and service observations using agreed low-impact methods.

Risk and business context

Technology and security signals: review public application clues, headers and selected email-domain controls.

Reporting and follow-through

Ownership and prioritization: distinguish confirmed organizational assets from uncertain third-party or shared infrastructure before recommending action.

Questions buyers ask about external attack surface assessment

Is an external attack surface assessment passive?

Some discovery sources are passive, while service checks may involve direct requests. The exact methods, rate limits and permitted targets must be agreed before assessment.

Can you scan any domain?

No. The client must establish ownership or provide authorization for the requested assessment. Public visibility does not grant permission for intrusive testing.

What is the difference between attack surface management and penetration testing?

Attack surface assessment focuses on discovering and understanding exposed assets. Penetration testing goes further by validating selected weaknesses through controlled testing.
